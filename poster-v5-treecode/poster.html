1 · The code in one line

Holographic perfect-tensor codes on a truncated Bruhat–Tits tree: a [[3,1,1]] perfect tensor sits at every internal vertex, logical information lives at the root, physical qubits at the leaves, and error correction is built into the tree geometry rather than applied by active measurement loops.

2 · Verification status (as of 21 Aug 2026)

Classical validation: done, three methods. Hardware: none yet — no tree code has run on a quantum processor; everything above is simulation.

First falsifiable prediction — tested, null. Log-periodic oscillations in the Planck 2018 temperature spectrum: no signal found at any resolvable radix (bootstrap p = 0.89); bispectrum: upper bounds only. The testing machinery runs and publishes nulls.

Published boundary. Under independent errors the qudit family sits at ≈ 2×10⁻⁴ (~55× below surface codes) — the architectural claim targets the correlated-failure regime, and that boundary is published, not hidden.

No-go engagement. The QEC–Darwinism tradeoff (F_L > 0.874) was audited on tree spaces: collective fragment refinement moves the boundary to F_L ≈ 0.83–0.85, residual gap disclosed.

Self-correction published. A naive p-adic valuation reading of stabilizer codes carries no new content; the program published the correction rather than the confirmation.

T1 and T2 are pure classical work — cheap, decisive, and doable immediately; they answer the two objections this room will raise first. Every criterion is numeric, so a null result is publishable.

4 · What we ask of this room

1. An openly specified correlated-noise benchmark (data + model) we can run T1 against.

2. Decoder reality check: does a known decoder family already cover tree-structured codes, and how does its complexity scale?

3. Pointers on perfect tensors for p > 2 (binary [[3,1,1]] is settled; larger primes are open).

4. Independent re-run of T1 — we hand over the simulator and the protocol; a third-party rerun settles the classical claim.
